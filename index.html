What is the Air Pollution Index (API)?

The Air Pollution Index (API) is a numerical scale used to measure the quality of the air and its impact on human health. It reflects the concentration of key pollutants, such as particulate matter (PM10, PM2.5), ozone (O₃), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), sulfur dioxide (SO₂), and carbon monoxide (CO). Higher API values indicate poorer air quality and greater potential health risks. Monitoring API helps the public stay informed about air pollution levels and take necessary precautions.

API Comparison: An Overview

API Shifts in Malaysia

The Air Pollution Index (API) of Peninsular Malaysia peaked at 56.40 in 2019, surpassing the national average of 53.51. Sabah and Sarawak maintain lower API values, ranging between 33 – 48, which pull down the overall Malaysia average.

After a sharp decline in 2020, likely linked to reduced human activity, Peninsular Malaysia’s API remains elevated compared to Sabah and Sarawak, while the national API mirrors the regional trends, staying between 43.84 and 45.64 from 2020 to 2022.

Overall, the diagram highlights the disproportionate contribution of Peninsular Malaysia to the country’s air pollution, while Sabah and Sarawak maintain relatively cleaner air.
