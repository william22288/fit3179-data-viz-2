The Air Pollution Index (API)

in Malaysia

This visualization provides an interactive overview of Malaysia’s air pollution landscape across recent years.

1
 Average Air Pollution Index by Region (2017–2022)

The grouped bar chart shows that Peninsular Malaysia consistently has a higher average Air Pollution Index (API) compared to Sabah and Sarawak from 2017 to 2022, indicating more severe air pollution in the peninsula. Sabah and Sarawak maintain lower and relatively similar API levels, with occasional fluctuations. Overall, API increased sharply in 2019 across all regions, before stabilising around 2020–2022. The slider allows users to focus on specific time periods, revealing trends and changes in air quality over the years.

2
 Regional and Overall API Comparison (2019–2022)

The Air Pollution Index (API) of Peninsular Malaysia peaked at 56.40 in 2019, surpassing the national average of 53.51. Sabah and Sarawak maintain lower API values, ranging between 33 – 48, which pull down the overall Malaysia average. After a sharp decline in 2020, likely linked to reduced human activity, Peninsular Malaysia’s API remains elevated compared to Sabah and Sarawak, while the national API mirrors the regional trends, staying between 43.84 and 45.64 from 2020 to 2022. Overall, the diagram highlights the disproportionate contribution of Peninsular Malaysia to the country’s air pollution, while Sabah and Sarawak maintain relatively cleaner air.

3
 Distribution of API Monitoring Stations by Region

The majority of air pollution index (API) monitoring stations are concentrated in Peninsular Malaysia, which hosts 73.8% of the total stations, while Sarawak and Sabah account for 16.9% and 9.2%, respectively. This indicates a regional imbalance in monitoring coverage, with the western region being far better monitored than the eastern states.

4
 Distribution of API Monitoring Stations by State

The second chart breaks down the distribution by state and federal territory, highlighting that Sarawak has the most stations (11, 16.9%), followed by Johor (9, 13.8%), while several smaller states and territories such as Perlis, Labuan, and Putrajaya have only one station each (1.5%). These visualisations reveal that API monitoring is unevenly distributed, with a concentration in more populous or industrialised regions.

5
 Average Yearly API Ranking by State (2017–2022)

The bump chart illustrates how the ranking of average yearly Air Pollution Index (API) values has shifted among Malaysian states and federal territories from 2017 to 2022. Areas such as Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, and Putrajaya consistently rank higher, indicating poorer air quality, while areas like Sarawak, Labuan, and Sabah tend to maintain lower API ranks, reflecting cleaner air. The variations in ranking over the years highlight regional differences in pollution levels and changing environmental conditions, showing that air quality is not uniform across Malaysia and can fluctuate significantly between states and years.

6
 Daily API Trends Across Selected Stations (2017–2022)

The multiline chart allows users to compare daily Air Pollution Index (API) trends across up to three selected monitoring stations from 2017 to 2022. By using the dropdown menus and year sliders, users can explore how air quality varies over time and between locations. The chart reveals fluctuations in pollution levels, seasonal patterns, and notable peaks or declines, enabling a clearer understanding of how different regions experience air quality changes throughout the years.

7
 Monthly Average API and Population Distribution (2020–2022)

The proportional symbol map visualizes the monthly average Air Pollution Index (API) across Malaysia from 2020 to 2022, showing both air quality and population distribution. Darker red circles indicate areas with higher air pollution levels, while larger circles represent more populated locations. By selecting different months through the dropdown menu, users can observe spatial and temporal variations in air quality, identifying pollution hotspots and seasonal patterns, particularly in densely populated urban regions.

8
 Maximum API Across Selected Stations (2020)

The bar chart compares the maximum Air Pollution Index (API) recorded at selected monitoring stations across Malaysia in 2020. Cheras, Kuala Lumpur shows the highest API at 132, indicating the most severe air pollution among the stations, while Kota Kinabalu, Sabah records the lowest at 77. Overall, stations in Peninsular Malaysia, such as Melaka, Johor Bahru, and Penang, generally exhibit higher maximum API values compared to those in East Malaysia, highlighting regional differences in air quality likely influenced by urban activity and population density.
